For a hundred years, the answer has been to let water in.

Concrete and masonry are full of tiny channels, and water finds them. So the industry learned to work around it: drain the basement, coat the floor, seal the driveway, and redo it when it fails.

The wall becomes the waterproofing.

Stage 1

Reactive Penetration

WetBond is water-reactive. Applied to concrete that's been pre-wetted, it drives into the concrete. Damp walls are where it does its best work.

Stage 2

Covalent Bonding

It doesn't stick to the wall. It bonds into it.

Coatings hold on by grip, and grip lets go. As WetBond reacts, it forms a network inside the pores and bonds chemically to the pore walls.

In plain terms: it locks onto the concrete itself.

Isn't this just a coating?

No. Coatings sit on the wall, and water pressure eventually pushes them off. WetBond doesn't sit on the wall. It's embedded in it.

Why coatings fail

Don't take our word for it. Watch the water.

And this is our lightest grade

One brick, two halves. One half gets WetBond, the other is left bare. Then we pour water on both, two hours later and again a week later. The bare half drinks it in. The treated half holds it on the surface.

This is WetBond Color, the entry grade in the family.

Bond type

Covalent

A true chemical bond, not just grip. WetBond reacts with the concrete inside its pores and forms covalent bonds with it, sharing electrons at the molecular level.

Most waterproofing fights moisture. WetBond uses it, reacting with the water inside the concrete and bonding into the concrete itself.

Works on the wall you have.

Old stone, brick or block foundations are often told drainage is the only option. The more porous the wall, the more WetBond goes on, and your dealer sets the amount for your wall.

Walls with crumbling mortar are repointed first. That's repair work WetBond goes over, not something it replaces.

Applied from the inside.

  1. Step 1

    Assess

    Your dealer inspects walls, floor and joints, takes moisture readings, and tells you exactly what your home needs.

  2. Step 2

    Repair

    Cracks, joints and tie-rod holes are opened and filled first.

  3. Step 3

    Prepare

    The concrete is cleaned, opened and pre-wetted, so WetBond can drive in behind the moisture.

  4. Step 4

    Apply

    WetBond is rolled and brushed on. It reacts with the moisture in the wall and works its way in.

  5. Step 5

    Document

    Your dealer records readings and photos and registers the job, activating your manufacturer warranty.

Most basements are a one-day application. Prep and repairs can add time, and your dealer will tell you how much up front.
