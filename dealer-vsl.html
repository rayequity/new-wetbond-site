01

Coatings sit on the wall. WetBond becomes the wall.

Every film, from paint to epoxy to a membrane, is held on by grip. Water moving through the concrete reaches the back of that film, pressure builds at the bond line, and the film blisters, bulges and lets go. That's why the industry gave up on stopping water and started draining it.

WetBond works the other way. The wall is pre-wetted, WetBond drives into the concrete and reacts there.

Floors work the same way. A coating on a damp slab fails from the moisture underneath; treat the slab with WetBond first and the finish goes onto concrete that keeps water out.

Now watch the water

And this is our lightest grade.

One brick, two halves. One half gets WetBond, the other is left bare. Water goes on at two hours and again a week later. The bare half drinks it in; the treated half holds it on the surface.

How a job gets done

Six steps, the same way every time.

Every Certified Dealer follows the same written protocol, and every step leaves something on the record. That record is what backs the warranty and what you show the next homeowner.

1

Assess

Moisture readings, photos of every problem area and a written scope the homeowner signs off on.

On the record: Readings and photos

2

Repair

Cracks and joints are routed open and filled, tie-rod holes patched, and Injection Gel goes in first wherever water is actively running.

On the record: What was repaired, and where

3

Clean and pre-wet

Efflorescence, paint and oils come off; the concrete is chemically cleaned and rinsed, then pre-wetted so it’s evenly damp.

On the record: Surface condition before application

4

Mix

Side A and Side B are combined on site in a clean pail, in the ratio and for the mix time the protocol sets.

On the record: Batch numbers for both sides

5

Apply

Rolled, brushed or squeegeed onto the walls, into the cove and across the floor at the set coverage, as one continuous envelope.

On the record: Area treated and gallons used

6

Document and register

After-readings and photos close the job, and the dealer registers it so the homeowner’s manufacturer warranty starts.

On the record: After-readings, photos, registration

04 Why the demand is there

Four things keep wet basements and slabs coming: more people finishing basements, which needs a dry envelope first; more garage and basement floors getting coated, and plenty of them fail the moisture test; standard homeowners policies, which typically exclude groundwater seepage, so the fix comes out of pocket; and water damage that gets worse and more expensive every season it's left.

Expect to sign non-circumvention agreements

When a referral partner does some of the same kind of work you do, such as a remodeler, a coating installer or a concrete contractor who sends you a wet basement or slab, expect to sign a non-circumvention agreement with them. You agree not to go after their customers for the work they do themselves, so a referral never costs them a client. It’s what lets contractors who can’t or don’t want to take on this work send it to you with confidence, and it’s how you become the contractor other trades in your city trust.

Referral relationships are yours to build, and the terms are between you and each partner. Whether and how you compensate referrals is up to you, and some professions, including home inspectors and real estate agents in many states, have rules about referrals and referral fees. Check yours before you set anything up.

Questions contractors ask

Before you apply.

You can't waterproof from the inside.

That rule is about surface films held on by grip. WetBond bonds into the concrete.

Nothing sticks to a wet slab.

Coatings don't. WetBond is water-reactive, so the substrate is pre-wetted on purpose and the material drives in.

What about hydrostatic pressure?

“Relieve the pressure” applies to membranes. Where groundwater is high, relief is added by condition, not by default.

My crew doesn't do waterproofing.

Certification covers it: online training, hands-on instruction, a written exam and review of your first documented jobs.

Will it work on block, brick or stone?

Yes. The application protocol sets the thickness by how porous the substrate is, so porous walls simply take more material.

Is this a franchise?

No. No franchise fee, territory fee or royalty, no trade name to carry and no say in how you run your business. You buy product.

What happens on the call?

About 30 minutes. We check whether your county is open, walk through the opening order, certification and warranty, and answer your questions. If it isn't a fit, we'll say so.

Do I need waterproofing or coatings experience?

No. Certification covers the system. Contractors who already work on concrete, slabs or walls pick it up fastest.

Can I start with floors instead of basements?

Yes. Slab and floor dealers can certify too.
